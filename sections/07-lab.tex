\section{Repeated Kubernetes/GitOps Laboratory}
\label{sec:lab}

The closed-world study fixes semantics; a live stack tests whether the
declared classes and evidence dependencies are concretely realizable through
control-plane interfaces. We therefore executed a repeated laboratory over
all nine scenarios and six benign controls. Its purpose is deliberately
bounded: realizability and within-laboratory detector behavior on one pinned
stack. It is not evidence of natural occurrence, field prevalence,
production effectiveness, or transfer across organizations.

\subsection{Build and Reproducibility Boundary}
A single-node Kind~v0.32.0 cluster running Kubernetes~v1.36.1~\cite{kind}
hosts Flux~v2.9.4~\cite{fluxcd} and Kyverno~v1.18.2~\cite{kyverno}. A local
smart-HTTP Git service drives Flux and a local OCI registry supports
tag/digest substitution. Approval records and the environment inventory are
versioned JSON files, not production approval or cloud-inventory services.
Bootstrap pins upstream manifests by SHA-256, preloads the required arm64
images, and records platform versions. An approved-state recorder snapshots
$\Gapp$: manifest, resolved pod-image digest, policy pin, approval JSON with
validity window, Git revision, and declared environment assumptions. The
dependency-free evaluator reads Kubernetes, Git, Kyverno PolicyReports,
file-backed approvals, pod image IDs, and the file-backed inventory through
tier-restricted entry points T0n--T4. The bootstrap, injections, repeated
harness, evaluator, raw observations, controls, and generated tables ship
under \texttt{lab/}.

\subsection{Protocol and Four Clocks}
For each repetition, a seeded shuffle changes scenario order. Before every
injection, the harness restores the approved Git revision, policy $\pi_7$,
approval record, artifact tag, deployment, and inventory, and requires T4 to
return \emph{consistent}. Each S1--S9 scenario is executed 20 times. Three
logical evaluators observe the same injection at cadences 0.5, 2, and 10~s;
their first post-onset phase is sampled independently and deterministically
from $[0,c)$ for cadence $c$, after which they poll every $c$ seconds. Thus a
latency below 0.5~s is possible when the first scheduled poll falls shortly
after onset; 0.5~s is a cadence, not a measurement-resolution floor. We
report two decimal places and retain higher-resolution raw clocks only for
recalculation.

The harness records four timestamps: command initiation
$t_{\mathrm{inject}}$, operational inconsistency
$t_{\mathrm{onset}}$, first tier-appropriate verdict
$t_{\mathrm{detect}}$, and completion of the minimal JSON evidence bundle
$t_{\mathrm{evidence}}$. It reports actuation, DDL, end-to-end latency, and
TTE exactly as defined in \cref{met:ddl}. For command-carried mutations,
onset is conservatively operationalized as successful command completion.
For S2 it is exception expiry; for S4, successful rollout of the substituted
artifact; and for S6, successful Flux convergence to the unapproved revision.
Consequently, S6's Git commit and reconciliation belong to actuation and
end-to-end time, not DDL. S3 begins at successful policy supersession, so its
DDL includes waiting for Kyverno's background PolicyReport. Evidence
assembly in this reference implementation is synchronous with verdict
serialization, so TTE and DDL are nearly identical; this does not estimate
retrieval delay for a platform-scale evidence service.

Ground truth is a set. S6 is $\{\text{intent},\text{authorization}\}$; the T2
evaluator's first-priority verdict is intent, which is scored as a member of
that set rather than by simplifying the ground truth. Detection rate (DR)
counts a non-consistent verdict before the 180-s timeout. Classification
accuracy (CA) is conditional on detection.

\subsection{Detection, Classification, and Cadence}
At the default 0.5-s cadence, all 180 injections were detected and all
detected verdicts belonged to their expected class sets
(\cref{tab:lab-results}). The separation of clocks changes the interpretation
materially. For S6, median actuation was 10.05~s, median DDL 0.47~s, and
median end-to-end latency 10.52~s. The former one-pass value around 7~s was
therefore not detector latency. Conversely, S3 had median actuation 0.15~s
but median DDL 7.98~s (P95 10.25~s), exposing the background-policy path.

Across all cadences, 538 of 540 observations produced a verdict, and all 538
detected observations were classified correctly. Both misses were S1 in the
same repetition: the 0.5-s evaluator observed the manual mutation, but Flux
reconciled it before the first 2-s and 10-s polls. This is not discarded as
an outlier. It demonstrates the cadence limit predicted by \textbf{LH2}:
polling a transient relation can miss it even when classification is perfect
when observed.

\Cref{tab:lab-cadence} reports the aggregate effect. DR was 100.0\% at 0.5~s
and 99.4\% at both 2 and 10~s. Conditional CA remained 100.0\%. Median DDL
rose from 0.46 to 1.46 and 6.11~s as cadence widened; P95 rose from 8.06 to
8.37 and 12.36~s. P95 is dominated partly by S3's PolicyReport delay rather
than cadence alone, so these are distributions of the complete deciding
paths on this laboratory, not calibrated evaluator-service times.

\subsection{Benign Live-Stack Controls}
The positive scenarios do not establish specificity. We therefore ran 20
10.5-s drift-free windows, balanced round-robin across six controls:
satisfied policy revision (C1), rollback accompanied by approval for the new
revision (C2), exception removal at expiry (C3), re-tag accompanied by
approval for the new digest (C4), autoscaling replica change outside the
managed projection (C5), and a legitimate rollout restart (C6). Every window
was evaluated at all three cadences.

The controls produced 543 evaluator polls and zero alarms
(\cref{tab:lab-controls}). This supports \textbf{LH3} within the declared
projection: the evaluator did not simply classify every change as drift.
It does not bound false-positive rates under untested production churn; with
zero observed alarms, the defensible statement is $0/543$ polls and $0/20$
windows, not a claim of zero population probability.

\subsection{Evidence Boundary and Refutation Conditions}
The repeated execution strengthens the earlier one-pass realizability check
but remains controlled and single-cluster. It uses injected scenarios,
file-backed approval and environment streams, a single deployment, no
concurrent workload churn during positive injections, and no induced stream
loss or API saturation. It estimates a distribution only for this pinned
laboratory protocol.

The expected tier/class shape appeared whenever an injection was observed,
supporting the realizability part of \textbf{LH1}. The two S1 misses support
the cadence-bounded qualification in \textbf{LH2} and refute any stronger
claim of cadence-independent detection. Zero alarms in the six benign change
families support \textbf{LH3} within scope. \textbf{LH4} remains open: the
minimal evidence bundle is constructed synchronously after verdict, so this
laboratory does not test a separate, platform-bound evidence retrieval path.
Misclassification of an observed higher-tier scenario, alarms under these
benign controls, or a repeatable class requiring less evidence than
\cref{prop:tiers} states would refute the corresponding claims.
