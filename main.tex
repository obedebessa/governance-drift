% ------------------------- abstract ----------------------------------
\noindent\textbf{\textit{Abstract}---Configuration drift is a two-place, memoryless comparison of desired and observed configuration. We define \emph{governance drift} as a three-place, history-anchored relation: operational reality diverges from its current governance context and the immutable basis under which it was approved, or the evidence needed to decide that relation is lost. A resource can match Git while violating current policy, relying on expired authority, or running unapproved content. We contribute a five-component governance-divergence vector plus an epistemic mask; an append-only approval-snapshot model; separation and tier-minimality results; and a cumulative detection architecture. In a twelve-scenario closed world, complete-set scoring realizes the predicted staircase; an independent union of plane-local signals exactly diagnoses 9/12 scenario sets versus 12/12 for the admitted-basis join. In a pinned Kind/Flux/Kyverno laboratory, nine scenarios were each repeated 20 times at randomized-phase 0.5-, 2-, and 10-second cadences. The evaluator detected 538/540 observations with 100\% conditional exact-set accuracy; a cluster bootstrap over 180 injections gives a 95\% interval of 98.89--100\% for aggregate detection. Twenty benign windows produced 0/543 poll alarms. A secondary live extension completed the exact vectors in 45/45 compound observations, and a 30-minute benign soak produced 0 alarms or epistemic warnings in 4,680 polls. Separate actuation, detection, vector-completion, and evidence clocks retain two transient misses and asynchronous partial diagnoses. This bounded execution demonstrates realizability on one stack with file-backed governance streams; it does not estimate prevalence, production effectiveness, transport reliability, or transfer.}
